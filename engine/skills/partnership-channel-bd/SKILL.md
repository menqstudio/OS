# Partnership Channel BD

## Trigger
Use when the task is to evaluate, structure, or operationalize partnerships: tech/integration alliances, reseller/VAR and distribution channels, referral/affiliate programs, OEM/embed deals, or co-marketing/co-sell motions. Also for partner tiering, enablement, and deal-registration design. Do NOT use for direct-sales execution (route to sales-revenue-growth) or binding contract terms (route to legal-compliance-contracts).

## Inputs
- Partner candidate(s): overlap of ICP, complementary vs. competitive stance, reach, and credibility.
- Partnership type and the mutual value hypothesis (what each side gives and gets).
- Economics: margin/rev-share, MDF, quota/commit, and cost to serve.
- Strategic goal: which gap the partnership fills (reach, product, credibility, geography).

## Workflow
1. Qualify fit: score candidate on ICP overlap, complementarity (not competition), reach, and mutual incentive; kill poor fits early.
2. Define the value exchange explicitly for BOTH sides; a partnership with one-sided value will not survive — state each party's win.
3. Choose the model: referral (low commit), reseller (margin + channel conflict risk), tech integration (product depth), or co-sell (joint pipeline); match to goal and cost to serve.
4. Design economics: rev-share/margin tiers, deal registration to prevent channel conflict, MDF terms, and any commit/quota — model partner-side profitability, not just yours.
5. Plan enablement: what the partner needs to succeed — training, collateral, sandbox, sales support — and the ramp timeline to first joint win.
6. Set governance: named owners on both sides, cadence, shared pipeline/attribution tracking, and clear rules for conflict resolution.
7. Define success metrics and a review/exit clause: sourced/influenced pipeline, activation rate, time-to-first-deal, and the threshold that continues or sunsets the partnership.

## Outputs
- Partner qualification scorecard and go/no-go recommendation.
- Two-sided value proposition and chosen partnership model with rationale.
- Economic model (rev-share/margin, MDF, deal-reg rules) and channel-conflict safeguards.
- Enablement plan and governance/metrics framework with review/exit criteria.

## Safety limits
No scope expansion, secret access, credential handling, push, merge, deployment, deletion, external communication, or production mutation without the exact governing grant and approval boundary. Never sign partner agreements, commit rev-share, share roadmap/customer data, or contact partners without grant. Do not fabricate partner interest or economics. Ambiguous mutation targets fail closed.

## Handoffs
Binding partner agreements and rev-share terms to legal-compliance-contracts. Direct-deal execution and quota to sales-revenue-growth. Co-marketing assets to marketing-brand. Escalate cross-domain decisions to the owning SST role; medium, high, and critical work requires an independent verifier; outreach/signing hands off only to the authorized executor.

## Verification
Confirm the value exchange is explicitly two-sided, partner economics are modeled (not just first-party), channel-conflict and deal-registration safeguards exist, enablement and governance owners are named, and success/exit metrics are defined. Any claimed partner commitment without evidence remains RED.

## Failure and rollback
Stop on one-sided value, unmodeled partner economics, undefined channel-conflict rules, or a request to contact/sign without grant. Discard the proposal, restore prior state, and report the missing input. Never mark an unsigned or one-sided partnership GREEN.
